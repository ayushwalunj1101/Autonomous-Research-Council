01 Executive Summary
Federated learning with differential privacy (ε=1.0, δ=1e-5) can substantially reduce re-identification risk in multi-hospital ML pipelines, while retaining model accuracy within acceptable margins of centralised baselines. The proposed methodology employs personalized federated learning (pFedMe) to address non-IID data distributions across hospital sites, and FedProx (μ=0.01) with noise-aware loss weighting to counter 35% label noise introduced by inconsistent annotation protocols. Validation is proposed on MIMIC-III with synthetic noise injection across three benchmark tasks.

03 Strengths & Weaknesses
✓ Strengths
→ pFedMe directly addresses the non-IID distribution problem across heterogeneous hospital datasets, with strong prior empirical support.
→ Differential privacy budget is explicitly defined (ε=1.0, δ=1e-5), making the privacy claim falsifiable and auditable by regulators.
→ FedProx with noise-aware loss weighting is a proven robust aggregation strategy for noisy federated environments.
→ MIMIC-III is a widely accepted clinical benchmark, ensuring comparability with prior literature.
✗ Weaknesses & Risks
→ No proposed mechanism for hyperparameter tuning across federated nodes without central coordination — requires further design.
→ Privacy-accuracy tradeoff at ε=1.0 may be insufficient for highly sensitive clinical sub-tasks — sensitivity analysis needed.
→ 35% label noise assumption may not generalise across all hospital annotation workflows — scope should be stated explicitly.
→ Communication overhead of federated rounds is not addressed — critical for real-world hospital network constraints.
04 Chaos Protocol Response

The methodology is assessed as robust to this constraint — the core privacy-preserving claims remain valid under noisy conditions.
05 Proposed Methodology
1
Dataset Preparation & Noise Characterisation
Partition MIMIC-III across 5 simulated hospital nodes. Inject synthetic label noise (15–40% range) per node. Estimate per-node noise rate using pre-training on a small clean validation set.
2
Federated Training with Differential Privacy
Apply pFedMe with personalised local models per node to handle non-IID distributions. Add Gaussian noise mechanism (ε=1.0, δ=1e-5) to gradients before aggregation.
3
Robust Aggregation via FedProx
Use FedProx (μ=0.01) with noise-aware loss weighting to down-weight high-noise nodes during global model aggregation. Validate convergence across 100 federation rounds.
4
Privacy-Accuracy Tradeoff Analysis
Sweep ε ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0} to characterise the privacy-accuracy frontier. Report AUC, F1, and re-identification risk score for each configuration.
5
Benchmark Comparison & Reporting
Compare against centralised baseline, vanilla FedAvg, and DP-FedAvg on 3 MIMIC-III tasks. Report with 95% CI over 5 random seeds. Target venues: NeurIPS, ICLR ML4Health workshop.
